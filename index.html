What an A&P technician can handle

An FAA Airframe and Powerplant mechanic is authorized to inspect, maintain, and approve for return to service a broad range of general aviation work. Kinney Aviation’s CEO holds both ratings — Airframe (A) and Powerplant (P) — plus a pilot certificate, so maintenance and ferry flight stay under one accountable professional.

A

Airframe rating

Covers the aircraft structure and systems other than the powerplant.

  • Sheet metal, riveted, and composite structural repairs
  • Landing gear, wheels, brakes, and shock struts
  • Flight controls, cables, pushrods, and rigging
  • Doors, windows, fairings, and exterior finishes
  • Cabin, environmental, and pressurization-related systems (as equipped)
  • Pitot-static, electrical, and avionics mounting support (with appropriate authorizations)
  • Airframe airworthiness directives and service bulletins
P

Powerplant rating

Covers engines, propellers, and the systems that make them run.

  • Reciprocating and turbine engine inspection & maintenance
  • Cylinder, magneto, and accessory work within rating scope
  • Fuel metering, induction, exhaust, and ignition systems
  • Propeller inspection, dressing, and governor support
  • Oil systems, cooling, and engine controls
  • Engine removals / installations and ground runs
  • Powerplant ADs, SBs, and life-limited parts tracking
A&P

Combined A&P capability

Holding both ratings means one mechanic can sign off a complete annual or 100-hour inspection, coordinate airframe and engine findings, and return the whole aircraft to service without handing work between separate shops — reducing schedule friction and duplicate diagnostics.
